## Source context

Throughout Section 1.15, `H` is a **complex Hilbert space** and `B(H)` is the
algebra of all bounded linear operators on `H`.

Immediately after Proposition 1.15.1, Sakai declares that a self-adjoint
subalgebra closed in the weak operator topology will be called a *weakly
closed self-adjoint subalgebra*. Proposition 1.15.2 assumes that `N` is such a
subalgebra of `B(H)` and lets `N_*` be the predual of `N`.

The source does not add an explicit unitality hypothesis. Proposition 1.15.1
and the preceding dual-space discussion make a weakly closed self-adjoint
subalgebra a W-star algebra, hence unital as a consequence. A formalization
should therefore retain the possibly nonunital input type and derive the
needed closed C-star and W-star structures instead of strengthening the source
hypothesis silently.

The concrete WOT, sigma-WOT, SOT, and strongest-operator topologies in the
statement are the restrictions to `N` of the topologies just defined on
`B(H)`. The topologies `sigma(N,N_*)` and `s(N,N_*)` are intrinsic to the
predual of `N`.

## Exact statement

For a weakly closed self-adjoint subalgebra `N` of `B(H)`, with predual
`N_*`, the following restricted topologies are equivalent on bounded spheres.

### Weak family

1. the weak operator topology;
2. the concrete sigma-weak operator topology generated by separately
   square-summable coefficient series;
3. the weak topology `sigma(N,N_*)` induced by the predual of `N`.

### Strong family

1. the strong operator topology;
2. Sakai's *strongest operator topology*, now usually called the concrete
   ultrastrong operator topology;
3. the intrinsic strong topology `s(N,N_*)`.

This is a theorem about equality of the **induced topologies on bounded
sets**. It is stronger than agreement of closed subalgebras and weaker than
global equality of the ambient topologies.

## Meaning of “bounded spheres”

Sakai's “sphere” terminology in this passage means a norm-closed ball, not a
metric sphere of points having norm exactly equal to a radius.

This interpretation is forced by the proof and its immediate context:

- on printed page 34 Sakai calls the norm unit ball the “unit sphere” while
  using its polar and compactness properties;
- in the proof of Proposition 1.15.1 he calls the set occurring in
  Kaplansky density a unit sphere, although the applicable theorem is the
  closed-unit-ball theorem;
- in the proof of Proposition 1.15.2 he invokes
  `sigma(N,N_*)`-compactness of the unit sphere, which is the Banach--Alaoglu
  compactness of the closed unit ball. The actual norm-one sphere is not
  weak-star compact in the required sense in general.

The primary formal target should therefore state equality, or explicit
homeomorphic identity maps, on each zero-centered closed ball

```text
{x : N | ||x|| <= C},    0 <= C.
```

Equality on an arbitrary norm-bounded subset then follows by restricting from
a containing closed ball. Translation invariance can provide corresponding
statements for other centers if a consumer needs them.

## Arbitrary nets, not sequences

The source proof writes an arbitrary net `x_alpha`. The proposition is a
topological statement and is not restricted to first-countable or metrizable
bounded sets. A sequence-only convergence theorem would therefore be an
uncertified weakening.

Suitable formal interfaces include either:

- equality/homeomorphism of the restricted topology-bearing carriers on each
  closed ball; or
- filter-general `Tendsto` equivalences for an arbitrary index type and
  filter, under an eventual common norm bound.

The topology-level closed-ball statement should be primary. Filter-general
and net-specialized convergence statements can then be derived as ergonomic
corollaries.

## Source proof

### Part 1: the weak-family compact sandwich

On `N`, the source uses the chain

```text
WOT <= concrete sigma-WOT <= sigma(N,N_*),
```

where the topology on the left is weaker. The closed unit ball of `N` is
compact for `sigma(N,N_*)` by Banach--Alaoglu, and WOT is Hausdorff. Hence the
continuous identity from the compact strongest realization of the ball to
the Hausdorff weakest realization is a homeomorphism. The intermediate
concrete sigma-WOT realization has the same induced topology. Scaling gives
the result on every bounded closed ball.

The second comparison in the displayed chain uses only that every concrete
coefficient-series functional on `B(H)` restricts to an element of `N_*`. It
does not require the later theorem representing every predual functional by
a coefficient series.

### Part 2: pass to positive squares

Sakai records the key equivalence

```text
x_alpha -> x in SOT
  iff
(x_alpha - x)^* (x_alpha - x) -> 0 in WOT.
```

Indeed, diagonal vector coefficients of the positive square are exactly the
squared pointwise norms. Polarization supplies the remaining WOT
coefficients.

If `x_alpha` and `x` lie in a common norm-bounded set, then the positive
square family is also norm bounded. Part 1 can therefore be applied to this
family. The defining seminorms give the corresponding translations:

- concrete ultrastrong seminorms of `x_alpha - x` are diagonal
  square-summable coefficient-series evaluations of its positive square;
- intrinsic strong seminorms are positive `N_*`-functional evaluations of
  the same square.

Thus WOT, concrete sigma-WOT, and `sigma(N,N_*)` convergence of the positive
squares yield SOT, concrete ultrastrong, and `s(N,N_*)` convergence of the
original bounded net. The decomposition of normal functionals into positive
parts, developed before Section 1.15, justifies testing the weak convergence
of positive squares through positive functionals.

## The predual `N_*`

The proposition says “the predual of `N`” without displaying a carrier. The
preceding paragraph constructs a weakly closed self-adjoint subalgebra as a
dual Banach space inside `B(H)`. The source-compatible concrete realization is
therefore the quotient of the ambient predual by the preannihilator of `N`:

```text
P_H / preannihilator(N),
```

where `P_H` is the concrete vector-functional predual of `B(H)`.

Sak-AI already has the relevant general infrastructure:

- `Ultraweak.preannihilator`;
- `Ultraweak.closedSubmoduleEquivAnnihilator`;
- `Ultraweak.closedSubmoduleEquivDual`;
- `Ultraweak.closedSubmodulePredual`;
- `Predual.congr` for transporting a specified predual across an explicit
  linear isometric equivalence;
- `Ultraweak.isCompact_closedBall` for Banach--Alaoglu compactness.

The first implementation should use this induced quotient predual because it
makes restriction of ambient normal functionals explicit. A later corollary
may transport the result to `WStarAlgebra.predual N` or to any other specified
predual by predual uniqueness. The proof should not depend on a chosen
predual whose relationship with restriction from `B(H)` is hidden.

The shortest missing general lemma is the evaluation formula

```text
(closedSubmoduleEquivDual N hN x) (Submodule.Quotient.mk p)
  = Predual.equivDual x.1 p.
```

Together with quotient induction, this packages `sigma(N, P / N_perp)` as a
continuous linear equivalence with the subtype
`Ultraweak.ofSubmodule (P := P) N` of ambient `sigma(M,P)`. This seam belongs
at arbitrary `RCLike` closed-submodule generality in `Ultraweak.Dual`, not in
the bounded-operator proof. `IsStarProjection.Corner.inducedPredual` is an
existing project precedent for thin source-specific packaging of the same
quotient construction.

## Existing concrete topology infrastructure

The preceding Proposition 1.15.1 transaction supplies:

- Mathlib's `ContinuousLinearMapWOT` for WOT;
- Mathlib's `PointwiseConvergenceCLM` for SOT;
- `ContinuousLinearMap.vectorFunctionalSeriesSpan` and its `WeakBilin`
  realization for Sakai's concrete sigma-WOT;
- `SquareSummableConvergenceCLM` for the concrete strongest/ultrastrong
  topology;
- `ContinuousLinearMap.VectorFunctionalPredual` as the concrete predual
  `P_H` of `B(H)`;
- the one-way comparison maps among these carriers;
- `NonUnitalStarSubalgebra.operatorTopologyClosedness_tfae`, which proves
  that a WOT-closed self-adjoint subalgebra is ultraweakly closed relative to
  `P_H`.

The general bounded-test API also already contains
`WeakBilin.tendsto_of_denseRange_of_eventually_norm_le` and
`Ultraweak.SakaiInvariantTestSpace.continuousOn_toUltraweak_closedBall`.
These may be useful corollaries, but the compact-sandwich proof of Part 1 is
closer to the source and avoids proving the later coefficient-series
representation theorem.

Pinned Mathlib, the available later official Mathlib snapshot, original
LeanOA, and the configured LeanOA upstream contain no equivalent of
Proposition 1.15.2 and no packaged relative weak-star topology for a closed
dual subspace. Mathlib does supply the concrete WOT/SOT convergence APIs and
the generic compact-to-Hausdorff homeomorphism theorem. The quotient-dual
isometry, concrete predual, ultrastrong carrier, and induced-predual machinery
needed here are Sak-AI additions and should be reused rather than recreated.

## Missing APIs

No missing source theorem has been identified, but several carrier and
topology interfaces still need implementation.

1. **Induced-predual packaging for the algebra subtype.** The existing
   quotient-predual theorem is stated for a closed `Submodule` subtype. A
   public, carrier-safe equivalence or thin assembly theorem is needed for a
   `NonUnitalStarSubalgebra` subtype, without relying on definitional equality
   between the two subtype structures.

2. **Intrinsic-to-concrete weak restriction maps.** Construct continuous
   identity maps from `sigma(N,N_*)` to the restrictions of concrete
   sigma-WOT and WOT, with evaluation lemmas expressed through the quotient
   predual.

3. **Closed-ball carrier transport.** Package the same underlying norm ball
   across the topology-bearing synonyms and use compact-to-Hausdorff to obtain
   continuous equivalences or homeomorphisms. Avoid ad hoc repeated set
   extensionality in the final theorem.

4. **Positive-square SOT/WOT bridge.** Prove a reusable, filter-general theorem
   identifying pointwise/SOT convergence of `x_alpha` to `x` with WOT
   convergence of `(x_alpha - x)^* (x_alpha - x)` to zero. This should not
   assume sequentiality.

5. **Positive-square concrete ultrastrong bridge.** Relate the
   square-summable application seminorms to diagonal coefficient-series tests
   of the positive square on `N`.

6. **Positive-square intrinsic strong bridge.** Relate convergence in
   `s(N,N_*)` to convergence of the positive squares against positive
   predual functionals, then to `sigma(N,N_*)` convergence on the bounded
   square family.

7. **Intrinsic-strong restriction to concrete ultrastrong.** The existing
   ambient `B(H)` map uses `P_H`; the proposition needs the corresponding map
   from the intrinsic strong topology defined by the induced predual of `N`.

These are engineering and abstraction tasks, not evidence that the source
statement is uncertain.

## Forbidden overclaims

The implementation and public documentation must not claim any of the
following from Proposition 1.15.2:

- global equality of WOT, concrete sigma-WOT, and `sigma(N,N_*)`;
- global equality of SOT, concrete ultrastrong topology, and `s(N,N_*)`;
- equality of concrete sigma-WOT with the full ambient predual topology on all
  of `B(H)`;
- equality of concrete ultrastrong topology with intrinsic strong topology on
  all of `B(H)`;
- a trace-class, Hilbert--Schmidt, Schatten, or nuclear realization of any
  predual;
- a representation of every predual functional by one pair of
  square-summable vector sequences;
- a sequence-only result advertised as the source topology theorem;
- a theorem about the norm-one metric sphere substituted for the source's
  closed-ball statement;
- an additional unitality or factor hypothesis treated as though it appeared
  in the source.

The global concrete/intrinsic topology identifications occur only later in
Sakai, after the trace-class and coefficient-representation results, ending
with Corollary 1.15.6.

## Recommended bounded implementation split

### Transaction A — induced predual and weak-family bounded balls

1. Fix a WOT-closed `NonUnitalStarSubalgebra` `N` of `B(H)`.
2. Reuse Proposition 1.15.1 to obtain ambient ultraweak closedness.
3. Package the induced quotient predual of `N` through an explicit linear
   isometry between the algebra and submodule subtypes.
4. Construct the intrinsic-ultraweak-to-concrete-sigma-WOT-to-WOT identity
   maps.
5. Restrict them to each norm-closed ball and apply compact-to-Hausdorff.
6. Publish the weak-family topology equivalence and filter-general convergence
   corollaries.

This is the smallest source-faithful next transaction.

### Transaction B — positive squares and strong-family bounded balls

1. Prove the reusable SOT/positive-square-WOT convergence theorem.
2. Prove the concrete ultrastrong/diagonal-series square interface.
3. Prove the intrinsic strong/predual-positive square interface.
4. Apply Transaction A to the norm-bounded square family.
5. Publish the strong-family topology equivalence and its filter-general
   convergence corollaries.

### Transaction C — final source packaging and documentation

Package the two parts as the exact two-clause Proposition 1.15.2 theorem,
link it from Verso, and advance the source frontier only after both clauses
kernel-check. Any chosen-predual transport should be a corollary of the
induced quotient-predual theorem rather than a replacement for it.
