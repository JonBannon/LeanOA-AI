## Ambient space and hypotheses

At the beginning of §1.15, Sakai fixes a **complex Hilbert space** \(H\) and
writes \(B(H)\) for the algebra of all bounded linear operators on \(H\).

Proposition 1.15.1 assumes that \(N\) is a **self-adjoint subalgebra** of
\(B(H)\). The statement does not explicitly assume that \(N\) is unital. It is
not a statement about an arbitrary subset of \(B(H)\).

## The five topologies

Sakai defines the following topologies on all of \(B(H)\).

1. **Uniform topology.** This is the topology from the operator norm
   \[
   \lVert a\rVert=\sup_{\lVert\xi\rVert\leq 1}\lVert a\xi\rVert.
   \]

2. **Strong operator topology.** This is the Hausdorff locally convex topology
   generated by the seminorms
   \[
   a\longmapsto \lVert a\xi\rVert,\qquad \xi\in H.
   \]

3. **Strongest operator topology.** For every sequence
   \((\xi_i)\subseteq H\) satisfying
   \(\sum_i\lVert\xi_i\rVert^2<\infty\), Sakai uses the seminorm
   \[
   a\longmapsto
   \left(\sum_i\lVert a\xi_i\rVert^2\right)^{1/2}.
   \]
   The topology generated by all these seminorms is what the source literally
   calls the *strongest operator topology*. Modern terminology commonly calls
   this the **ultrastrong operator topology**.

4. **Weak operator topology.** This is the Hausdorff locally convex topology
   generated by
   \[
   a\longmapsto |(a\xi,\eta)|,\qquad \xi,\eta\in H.
   \]

5. **\(\sigma\)-weak operator topology.** For sequences
   \((\xi_n),(\eta_n)\subseteq H\) satisfying
   \[
   \sum_n\lVert\xi_n\rVert^2<\infty,
   \qquad
   \sum_n\lVert\eta_n\rVert^2<\infty,
   \]
   Sakai uses the seminorm
   \[
   a\longmapsto
   \left|\sum_n(a\xi_n,\eta_n)\right|.
   \]
   All such seminorms generate the \(\sigma\)-weak operator topology.

The uniform topology is part of the surrounding list, but it is not one of the
five closedness conditions in Proposition 1.15.1. Those five conditions use the
weak, \(\sigma\)-weak, strong, strongest, and predual weak topologies.

## Predual notation and the exact statement

Sakai lets \(V\) be the linear space of weak-operator-continuous linear
functionals on \(B(H)\), so the weak operator topology is
\(\sigma(B(H),V)\). He constructs the norm completion of \(V\) as the predual
\(B(H)_*\).

Proposition 1.15.1 says that, for a self-adjoint subalgebra \(N\subseteq B(H)\),
the following are equivalent: \(N\) is closed in

1. the weak operator topology;
2. the \(\sigma\)-weak operator topology;
3. the strong operator topology;
4. the strongest operator topology;
5. \(\sigma(B(H),B(H)_*)\).

The production predicates are, in the same order,
`NonUnitalStarSubalgebra.IsWOTClosed`, `IsSigmaWOTClosed`, `IsSOTClosed`,
`IsUSOTClosed`, and the established
`NonUnitalStarSubalgebra.IsUltraweakClosed` instantiated with the concrete
vector-functional predual. The ambient algebra is
`H →L[ℂ] H`, and `NonUnitalStarSubalgebra` preserves the source's lack of an
explicit unital assumption.

Condition (5) is the weak topology of the dual pair consisting of \(B(H)\) and
its predual—the topology now normally called the ultraweak or weak-star
topology. At this point in Sakai's exposition it must be distinguished from the
separately defined \(\sigma\)-weak operator topology in condition (2).
Proposition 1.15.1 proves only that the two topologies give the same closed
self-adjoint subalgebras. Their equality as topologies on all of \(B(H)\) is
stated later in Corollary 1.15.6.

## Global closedness versus bounded spheres

Proposition 1.15.1 is a **global closedness statement in \(B(H)\)**. It contains
no boundedness hypothesis and is not restricted to a ball or sphere.

The bounded-sphere statement is the next result, Proposition 1.15.2. For a
weakly closed self-adjoint subalgebra \(N\subseteq B(H)\), with predual \(N_*\),
it says:

1. the weak operator, \(\sigma\)-weak operator, and
   \(\sigma(N,N_*)\) topologies are equivalent on bounded spheres;
2. the strong operator, strongest operator, and \(s(N,N_*)\) topologies are
   equivalent on bounded spheres.

Consequently, bounded-set equivalence must not be inserted as an assumption or
conclusion of Proposition 1.15.1.

## Proof topology and dependencies

On printed page 35 (PDF page 47), Sakai uses the two chains
\[
\mathrm{WOT}
\leq \sigma\text{-}\mathrm{WOT}
\leq \sigma(B(H),B(H)_*)
\leq \tau(B(H),B(H)_*)
\]
and
\[
\mathrm{WOT}
\leq \mathrm{SOT}
\leq \text{strongest operator topology}
\leq s(B(H),B(H)_*)
\leq \tau(B(H),B(H)_*).
\]

For the first chain, a \(\sigma\)-weak coefficient functional is approximated
in operator norm by its finite partial sums. The estimate is the elementary
Cauchy–Schwarz tail estimate for the two square-summable vector sequences.

For the reverse closedness implication, Sakai assumes \(N\) is
\(\tau(B(H),B(H)_*)\)-closed and lets \(N_1\) be its
\(\sigma(B(H),V)\)-closure. The space \(V\) is norm-dense, self-adjoint, and
invariant in \(B(H)_*\). Kaplansky's density theorem then makes the unit sphere
of \(N\) \(\tau(B(H),B(H)_*)\)-dense in the unit sphere of \(N_1\); Mackey
closedness yields \(N_1=N\).

The source proof therefore depends on:

- Sakai's earlier dual-pair topologies \(\sigma\), \(s\), and \(\tau\);
- the Mackey theorem and the construction of \(B(H)_*\);
- norm density, self-adjointness, and invariance of \(V\) in the predual;
- Kaplansky's density theorem;
- the square-summable coefficient tail estimate.

## Terminology cautions

The expression **strongest operator topology** is a fixed topology name in
Sakai: it means the topology generated by the square-summable-vector seminorms
above. It is not definitionally the Mackey topology.

In the proof Sakai also says that \(\tau(B(H),B(H)_*)\) is “the strongest
topology.” There “strongest” describes the Mackey topology in its compatible
dual-pair role. This sentence must not be used to identify the Mackey topology
with the separately named strongest operator topology. Proposition 1.15.1 uses
only the comparison
\[
\text{strongest operator topology}
\leq s(B(H),B(H)_*)
\leq \tau(B(H),B(H)_*).
\]

The source also uses “unit sphere” in compactness and density passages where
modern accounts commonly formulate the result for the closed unit ball. Any
formalization of that auxiliary statement should check Sakai's convention or
use the precise modern Kaplansky-density formulation rather than relying on the
word alone.

## Remainder of §1.15 and the next frontier

The immediate exact numbered frontier after Proposition 1.15.1 is:

- **Proposition 1.15.2** (printed page 35, PDF page 47): equivalence of the
  indicated weak-family and strong-family topologies on bounded spheres of a
  weakly closed self-adjoint subalgebra.

The rest of the section then develops Hilbert–Schmidt and trace-class operators
(printed pages 35–38, PDF pages 47–50), followed on printed page 39 (PDF page
51) by:

- **Theorem 1.15.3:** isometric identification of \(B(H)_*\) with the trace-class
  operators, including positivity;
- **Corollary 1.15.4:** representation of normal positive functionals by a
  square-summable sequence of vector coefficients;
- **Corollary 1.15.5:** representation of arbitrary predual functionals by two
  square-summable vector sequences;
- **Corollary 1.15.6:** global identification of the strongest operator topology
  with \(s(B(H),B(H)_*)\), and of the \(\sigma\)-weak operator topology with
  \(\sigma(B(H),B(H)_*)\).

Thus Proposition 1.15.2, not the trace-class identification, is the next exact
numbered source checkpoint.
